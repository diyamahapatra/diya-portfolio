Product Design Internship · 2025

Flourish AI — Health Insights for GERD Patients

Designed and shipped a meal detail page and logging flow, explored AI-driven blood test insights with Gemini Deep Research, and navigated a fast-moving startup environment where product direction evolved quickly.

AI Feature Design Meal Logging UX User Research Design Systems Gemini LLM Prototyping
Timeline
Jun – Sept 2025
Role
Product Design Intern
Team
2 Designers · 1 PM
Tools
Figma · Gemini API
The Product

Helping users understand and manage their gut health

Flourish AI is a digital wellness app that uses food logging, AI, and health data integrations to help users understand gut health, identify triggers, and make informed lifestyle decisions.

Context

Logging and viewing meals is key

Every Flourish AI user needs to do two things well: log meals effortlessly and understand how those meals affect their symptoms. This became the foundation for Flourish AI's core meal tracking experience — and my job to lead.

Competitor Research

Understanding the gaps in existing nutrition apps

I analyzed 5 top platforms focused on meal logging and nutrition tracking, identifying what they did well and where they consistently fell short.

✦ Strengths across apps
  • Health trend visualizations
  • Personalized recommendations
  • Expert-backed guidance
  • Data-backed insights
✗ Consistent weaknesses
  • Overwhelming data density
  • High friction meal logging
  • Insights surfaced too late
☁️ How might we simplify the meal detail page for users while maximizing the information they can see — and reducing cognitive load?
User Research

I interviewed 15 users with various gut health conditions

I spoke with users managing GERD, IBS, diabetes, and other conditions to understand how they manage symptoms and where current apps fall short.

📄
Drowning in Data
Dense nutrition details make viewing insights overwhelming for users already managing a health condition.
👤
Personalization Drives Motivation
Users want trigger details tailored to their own symptoms — not generic nutritional averages.
🔗
No Cause → Effect
Users want clear signals linking what they ate to how they feel. That connection was missing in every app tested.
Ideation

I guided my team to 3 core ideas for the MVP

Based on user interviews and competitive research, I mapped features that would maximize personalization and insight — while minimizing effort and drop-off. All 3 were shipped.

💢
Irritant Score
A trigger score for each meal for ease of view — surfaces risk at a glance without requiring users to parse raw nutrition data.
✓ Shipped
📋
Multiple Logging Modes
Multiple ways to log a meal to reduce drop-off and simplify use across different user behaviors and contexts.
✓ Shipped
🫑
Macro Count Carousel
A visual carousel of daily macro progress — making nutrition scannable rather than something users have to calculate themselves.
✓ Shipped
MVP1 Feature Design

3 key enhancements to the meal detail page

1
Trigger Score
Translates complex nutrition and symptom data into a personalized, ingredient-level risk score. Powered by in-app AI (Flora), it explains why a meal may be triggering and suggests specific swaps tailored to each user's sensitivities.
2
Macro Visualization
Replaces dense text with intuitive visuals letting users quickly understand how a meal contributes to their daily goals — showing per-meal and daily macros side-by-side so users can contextualize meals without mental math.
3
Edit Logged Meal
Lets users easily update meals after logging, accommodating mistakes, late entries, or changes. This flexibility maintains trust in the data powering downstream insights.
Usability Testing

Macro count units were misleading to users

After the first round of testing, I changed the circular bars to horizontal bars to depict units more clearly — making it intuitive for users to track the scale of their meals without misreading the data.

Iteration

From start to finish — personalizable, visual, and informative

Initial Iteration
Little visualization or explanation — limited actionable insight for the user on how scores were calculated or what to do next.
Final Design — Shipped
Trigger scores with ingredient-level breakdowns, macro visualizations, and AI-powered suggestions surfaced inline. Owned end-to-end and shipped.
MVP2

Expanding into health data integrations

After shipping the core meal detail experience, I began exploring MVP2 — focused on integrating third-party health data (Apple Health, Oura, Whoop) to surface broader lifestyle trends alongside meal data.

My co-design intern and I explored lightweight homepage components that connect external data to gut health patterns at a glance — surfacing lifestyle metrics like sleep, steps, and heart rate variance alongside personalized daily tips, without overwhelming users with data density.

AI-Driven Features

Blood test insights, powered by Gemini Deep Research

Designed an AI feature that let users upload blood panels and get personalized, plain-language insight cards — cross-referenced with their meal logs.

🩸
Upload blood panel
PDF parsing flow for lab results — ferritin, CRP, B12, inflammatory markers
🤖
Gemini Deep Research
LLM cross-references biomarkers with logged meals + symptoms to find correlations
💡
Insight cards
Plain-language summaries with confidence levels, caveats, and next-step actions
↩️
Product pivot
Feature didn't ship — team shifted focus back to core meal logging. Classic startup. The prototype still shaped how I think about designing for AI-generated, probabilistic content.
Impact

My screen is featured on the App Store

Through a second round of usability testing with beta users, we measured meaningful improvements in how users understand and act on their meal data.

47%
reduction in time spent interpreting meal insights
68%
increase in users correctly identifying personal food triggers
1K+
users adopted the MVP at launch
Reflection

This internship sharpened my design and communication skills

✏️
Designing at scale within an existing system
Working within a large, established design system pushed me to think more intentionally about components, auto-layout, and variants — making thoughtful tradeoffs between consistency and clarity.
🔄
Adapting quickly in a fast-moving startup
As soon as MVP1 shipped, priorities shifted. This taught me how to balance shipping polished work while contributing to ambiguous, forward-looking initiatives without losing momentum.
💬
Communicating design decisions across disciplines
Collaborating closely with PMs and engineers strengthened my ability to articulate design rationale using research insights and tradeoffs — advocating for users while staying aligned with product goals.
🤖
Designing for AI outputs requires a different mindset
Working with Gemini Deep Research on the blood test insights feature taught me how to design around probabilistic, model-generated content — balancing usefulness with appropriate uncertainty, medical caveats, and trust-building. This is now a core part of how I approach any AI-driven feature.

Amazon Music — Designing new ways for creators and listeners to connect

In this designathon, our team explored how Amazon Music could evolve beyond traditional streaming by introducing global, interactive touchpoints that encourage discovery, participation, and community.

Concert UX Social Features Personalization Figma Prototyping Cross-Functional Collab
Timeline
Jun – Sept 2025
Role
UX Engineer
Team
4 Designers
Tools
Figma · Cross-Functional Collab
Overview

Amazon Music has expanded beyond streaming, but meaningful creator–fan engagement hasn't kept pace

While the platform now includes livestreams and creator tools, these experiences remain fragmented. Listeners lack clear ways to actively engage with creators, and creators have limited support for building sustained, global connections with their audience.

☁️ How might Amazon Music create shared, interactive experiences that allow listeners to actively engage with creators while giving creators better tools to build lasting global communities?
User Research

Understanding why users use Amazon Music and what they're primarily looking for

We conducted mixed-methods research across user sentiment, market patterns, and firsthand listening behaviors — combining large-scale survey analysis with qualitative depth to understand how listeners and creators currently engage with music platforms.

58/61
respondents were listeners, reinforcing that Amazon Music is primarily a consumption-driven platform
60+
listeners prioritized year-end summaries and curated playlists, while fewer than 15 engaged with livestreams
100%
of creators valued playlist engagement metrics and shared tools over follower counts
🎤
Listeners want richer interaction
Users want more than passive listening — they crave moments to feel connected to the artists they love.
🛠️
Creators lack centralized tools
Creators have no single place to build sustained, global communities — engagement is scattered across platforms.
Ideation

4 themes from affinity mapping shaped our design direction

After affinity mapping insights from listeners and creators, we identified recurring themes around connection, discovery, and engagement.

🤝
Feeling Close to Artists
Users should feel proximity to artists — not just consume their music. Experiences that humanize creators drive deeper loyalty.
🎵
Beyond the Concert
Users still want digital experiences that capture the energy of live moments, even from home.
💬
Low-Friction Social Interaction
Collaborative playlists and comments enable shared experiences without heavy social commitment.
🔍
Clear Discovery Paths
Users want easier ways to explore an artist's catalog and find songs aligned with their taste — not just algorithmic feeds.
Solution

Discover concerts you'll actually want to attend — together

We prototyped an Amazon Music live concert flow that personalizes event discovery based on a user's top artists and genres. We layered in social features like "Top Shared Artist" and friends' concert activity to turn passive listening into shared experiences.

1
View Upcoming Concerts
On your profile page, you can view upcoming live concerts, get tickets, enter the concert, and view the artist's setlist as you would in-person. See attendance counts and discover other popular concerts based on your listening history.
2
Friend Profile & Shared Artist Compatibility
By surfacing shared artists and friend activity, these screens personalize concert discovery and make live events feel more social. Users can quickly see which concerts align with their taste and where their friends are already engaged.
3
Live Concert Mode
Reimagines virtual performances as shared, interactive moments. Users switch between Live Mode and Lyrics Mode, react in real time, and chat with friends — recreating the collective energy of an in-person concert with synchronized lyrics and social interaction.
Usability Testing

Would users actually use our product?

We asked teams around us to test our Figma prototype and gathered their reactions. General consensus was positive — with some great suggestions.

⚡️
"I love the visuals!! They make the music feel alive, but I wish there was a way to customize them to match my mood."
🔄
"The shared artist compatibility score is pretty cool — maybe it'll be cooler if I can connect with fans attending the same concert."
🎨
"The live chat during concerts is engaging — it gets overwhelming when too many messages flood in. Maybe add filters?"
Reflection

This project holds a special place to me to this day

I loved working on something related to music in such a short amount of time, with a team of like-minded designers.

⏱️
Designing quickly and intentionally
This designathon pushed me to balance creativity with clear user needs under tight time constraints — a skill I carry into every project.
🎯
Personalization + social connection as leverage points
It reinforced how impactful thoughtful personalization and social connection can be, even when implemented through lightweight interactions.
🤝
Cross-functional collaboration under pressure
Working with 4 designers in a fast-paced environment taught me how to align quickly, divide work strategically, and synthesize feedback into a coherent vision.
