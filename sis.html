The Brief

How do you help 20,000 students find the right kind of advising on a site that can't ask them anything?

Three L&S advising pages and a routing flow, rebuilt for a platform that couldn't support a single interactive element — no quiz, no filter, no conditional logic. Just structure, language, and order.

Context

Students arrive with a situation, not a category.

A student doesn't wake up needing "College advising." They need to drop a class after the deadline, or figure out whether a summer course transfers, or ask whether their double major still works. The site was organized around the college's internal structure — the answer to a question nobody was asking.

Insights

Four themes from an affinity map, GA4, and a live site audit.

Walk through each method and what it surfaced — how many students, what the GA4 data showed about where people dropped off, and what the audit found.

What students needed

To be told the difference.

The distinction between College and Major/Minor advising, stated plainly rather than implied by page structure.

To know if they qualify.

Requirements surfaced before the request, not buried in prose at the bottom of the page.

To pick a moment, not a service.

A choice framed around time and urgency — a quick question now, or a scheduled appointment.

Affinity map

The Problem

Students had to understand how UC Berkeley's advising is structured before they could get help from it.

Advising options sat in dropdown sections students opened one at a time. The difference between College and Major/Minor advisers was never stated. Requirements that determine whether a request can even proceed were buried near the bottom of the page.

Before — the original page

Solution

Three pages, rebuilt around how students actually arrive.

Rather than explaining the college's structure more clearly, the redesign removes the need for students to understand it. It states distinctions the site had left implicit, deletes a choice that shouldn't have existed, and moves routing off the page entirely.
