Berkeley IT

Helping 20,000+ Berkeley students find the right advisor without having to understand Berkeley’s advising system first.

Role

Product Design Intern, UC Berkeley IT

Timeline

Summer 2026

Scope

Research, IA, interaction design, usability testing

Tools

Figma, Miro, Dovetail, Claude Code, Open Berkeley

Overview

Students had to understand how UC Berkeley’s advising is structured before they could get help from it.

L&S is Berkeley’s largest college, and its advising site supports everything from booking appointments to finding petitions and checking deadlines. Many of these tasks are time-sensitive, which made clarity especially important. But advising options were hidden across dropdowns students had to open one by one, the difference between College and Major/Minor advising was never made explicit, and eligibility requirements were buried near the bottom of the page.

I redesigned three high-priority advising experiences and a routing flow around what students were trying to accomplish, grounded in 261 survey responses and usability testing with nine students. Instead of explaining Berkeley’s advising structure more clearly, the designs reduce how much of that structure students need to understand in the first place.

Collaborators: Berkeley IT UX team + L&S Advising stakeholders

Projects

Four surfaces, rebuilt around student needs.

Appointments & Advice

Services grouped into clear sections, with College vs. Major/Minor advising compared side by side and special cases surfaced upfront.

Internal · not public

Virtual Front Desk

Two overlapping drop-in services consolidated into one, leaving students with a clearer choice between quick help and a scheduled appointment.

Internal · not public

Forms & Petitions

Forms reorganized with clearer categories and consistent descriptions so students can identify the right action without knowing institutional terminology.

Internal · not public

Routing Flow

A guided flow asks students about their situation and recommends the appropriate advising path instead of asking them to choose a service themselves.

Impact

What changed for students.

4 → 0

judgment calls. Students used to figure out which adviser they needed before they could book. Now they answer questions about their own situation.

2 → 1

services. We deleted a choice that shouldn’t have existed — both used the same form and the same Zoom link.

1

critical failure caught pre-launch. Seven of eight read an empty calendar as a broken page. We found it before students did.
